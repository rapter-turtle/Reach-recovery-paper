\author{Kiyong Park}

\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a robust cradle recovery framework based on reach avoid reachability for docking an underactuated unmanned surface vehicle (USV) into a cradle on the side of a moving mothership. Autonomous recovery is challenging because the underactuated vehicle must approach and enter a narrow cradle that advances with the mothership, without colliding with the hull or the cradle structure, while wind, wave, and current disturbances act on it. Safe and effective recovery therefore requires a controller that guarantees both collision avoidance and arrival at the recovery point, and Hamilton Jacobi reachability, in particular its reach avoid formulation, provides this guarantee by characterizing the set of states from which the vehicle can be driven into the cradle while avoiding collision under the worst case disturbance, together with the optimal control input that achieves it. Conventional dynamic programming solvers of this set are intractable in the high dimensional state space of a full vehicle model, whereas reinforcement learning makes the computation feasible. Building on this, the recovery set is learned through a robust reinforcement learning scheme, and a two phase recovery system is proposed, in which a disturbance observer based model predictive controller drives the vehicle during the approach from a far distance and the learned policy takes over to complete the recovery once the state enters the set. Numerical simulations and processor in the loop experiments demonstrate robust and collision free recovery under environmental disturbances.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec_intro}

Collaborative maritime systems that pair a large mothership with a fleet of small unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) are increasingly deployed for missions such as surveillance, search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and supply. In these systems the mothership serves as a mobile base that carries the small vehicles to the operating area, provides energy, communication, and maintenance support, and retrieves them when the mission ends. This arrangement is commonly described as a marsupial architecture, in which the mothership deploys and recovers the smaller vehicles through a dedicated launch and recovery system (LARS). Because manual retrieval of a small vessel alongside a moving ship is slow, labor intensive, and hazardous in a seaway, autonomous launch and recovery is essential for practical USV operation. Among the recovery configurations reported in practice, a cradle mounted on the side of the mothership is widely adopted, since it offers structural simplicity, keeps the recovered vehicle clear of the propulsion wake, and integrates naturally with existing shipboard handling equipment.

Autonomous recovery into such a cradle is a demanding control problem. The USV considered here is underactuated, driven by a single stern thruster and a rudder, so the sway direction carries no direct actuation and the thrust and rudder rates are limited. With this restricted authority the vehicle must approach and enter a narrow cradle pocket that advances with the mothership, so the recovery window is finite and the target keeps moving throughout the maneuver. Entry requires simultaneous alignment in position and heading, since a misaligned approach either misses the pocket or strikes its walls, and throughout the approach the vehicle must avoid collision with the mothership hull and the cradle structure. All of this must be achieved under persistent wind, wave, and current disturbances that continually push the vehicle off its intended path.

Safe and effective recovery therefore requires a controller that guarantees two properties at once, collision avoidance during the approach and arrival at the cradle within the available window. Two representative robust approaches fall short of this dual guarantee. Tube model predictive control keeps the state inside a tube around a nominal trajectory by means of a robust invariant set, but for a nonlinear underactuated vessel this invariant set must be constructed offline over the whole maneuvering envelope, the resulting tube tends to be conservative, and the method enforces constraint satisfaction along the nominal plan rather than guaranteeing entry into a moving target. Robust adaptive control barrier functions tighten a barrier condition by a disturbance bound and enforce safety as forward invariance one step at a time, which presumes that an admissible safe input exists at the current instant. Under underactuation and actuator saturation, however, this admissible input set can become empty before the cradle is reached, and recursive feasibility is not guaranteed. The authors' own prior recovery design used a funnel shaped robust adaptive control barrier function and shares this limitation \citep{park2026recovery}.

Hamilton Jacobi reachability, in particular its reach avoid formulation, directly provides the missing guarantee. The reach avoid set characterizes exactly those states from which the vehicle can be driven into the cradle while avoiding collision under the worst case admissible disturbance, and the associated value function yields the optimal control input that achieves this outcome. Membership in the reach avoid set is therefore a certificate that an admissible recovering input exists at every instant along the remaining maneuver, which is precisely the property that the barrier based methods cannot ensure.

Computing this set is the remaining obstacle. Classical grid based dynamic programming solvers of the reach avoid value discretize the state space and suffer from the curse of dimensionality, so they are intractable for the full state of the vehicle. Reinforcement learning removes this bottleneck by approximating the reach avoid value function and its policy with neural networks at this scale, and formulating the learning problem as a bounded disturbance game between control and disturbance is what makes the resulting set robust rather than merely nominal.

This paper builds on these observations and proposes a certified two phase recovery system for cradle recovery of an underactuated USV. A robust reinforcement learning scheme learns the reach avoid set and its policy for the vehicle under bounded disturbances. During the approach from a far distance, a disturbance observer based model predictive controller drives the vehicle toward the cradle \citep{park2026recovery}. Once the measured state enters the learned reach avoid set, the learned policy takes over and completes the docking. Set membership serves as the certificate on both sides of the switch, since it triggers the handoff into the docking phase and, if disturbances expel the state from the set, it triggers the fallback to the approach controller.

The contributions of this paper are twofold.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep=0pt
\item A robust discounted reach avoid Bellman formulation is developed that extends the discounted reach avoid backup to a bounded disturbance game and preserves the under approximation property and convergence to the true reach avoid set as the discount tends to one.
\item A certified two phase recovery framework is presented in which reach avoid set membership decides both the handoff to docking and the fallback, replacing an earlier dwell time heuristic.
\end{enumerate}
The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 reviews related work. Section 3 describes the vehicle model. Section 4 formulates the recovery problem. Section 5 presents the control system design. Section 6 reports the results. Section 7 concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec_related}

\textit{Hamilton Jacobi reachability and its learning based approximation.} HJ reachability characterises reach, avoid, and reach avoid sets as sublevel sets of the value function of a differential game \citep{lygeros2004reachability, mitchell2005time, margellos2011hamilton}, including settings with time varying dynamics and constraints \citep{fisac2015reach}, and a broad overview is given by \citet{bansal2017hamilton}. Grid based solvers deliver convergent approximations but scale exponentially with dimension. DeepReach replaces the grid with a self supervised neural PDE solver \citep{bansal2021deepreach}, and its outputs can be probabilistically verified through scenario optimization \citep{lin2023generating}. In our setting, however, a DeepReach style solver proved unreliable. It declared most of the state domain reachable while the policy extracted from its value gradients almost never completed the docking, and this failure motivates the reinforcement learning route pursued here, in which the value function and the policy are trained together so that the learned value is certified by a policy that actually attains it.

\textit{Reach avoid and safe reinforcement learning.} \citet{fisac2019bridging} showed that discounting the HJ safety backup yields a contraction whose fixed point under approximates the safe set and converges to it as the discount tends to one. \citet{hsu2021safety} generalised this construction from pure avoidance to reach avoid objectives, and \citet{yu2022reachability} used a learned reachability value as a constraint on a performance objective. Recent safe reinforcement learning estimates a feasible or reachable set, optimizes reward inside it, and minimises violation outside it \citep{ganai2023respo}. These methods are, however, largely developed without a bounded disturbance adversary, and to our knowledge no prior work combines an adversarial disturbance game with the discounted reach avoid backup. Docking under bounded sea disturbances requires precisely this combination, and this paper provides it together with a convergence argument.

\textit{Robust predictive and barrier based safety.} Robust model predictive control keeps the state inside a tube around a nominal trajectory through an ancillary control law and a robust invariant set \citep{langson2004tube}, but such invariant sets are difficult to construct and conservative for nonlinear underactuated vessels. Control barrier functions enforce safety as forward invariance and preserve it in an input to state safety sense under bounded perturbations \citep{xu2015robustness}, and related constructions include robust control barrier value functions \citep{choi2021robust} and HJ based refinement of hand designed barriers \citep{tonkens2022refining}. HJ values have also served as safety filters \citep{borquez2024safety} and as constraints inside model predictive control \citep{wang2025safety, zeng2021safety}. These approaches share a common limitation. They are predominantly avoid only, they enforce safety one step at a time or filter a nominal controller, and they therefore do not by themselves guarantee reaching a moving target and can lose feasibility under underactuation and actuator saturation.

\textit{Autonomous docking, berthing, and marine recovery.} Optimization based docking and berthing of surface vessels has been demonstrated with direct optimal control and model predictive control, including an optimization based collision free planner combined with dynamic positioning tracking validated in full scale experiments \citep{martinsen2019docking, bitar2020docking}, and end to end reinforcement learning has achieved autonomous docking of surface vehicles in unknown environments with validation on real platforms \citep{pereira2024illegal}. Reachability analysis has been applied to ship docking corridors against a static berth \citep{park2020reachability}. Underactuated marine craft guidance relies on adaptive line of sight path following with sideslip compensation of drift forces \citep{fossen2015los} and on disturbance observer based compensation of environmental loads in dynamic positioning \citep{tomera2021dob}, with standard vessel models following \citet{fossen2011handbook}. Launch and recovery to a platform has mostly been studied for underwater vehicles returning to a mother ship, including a docking and recovery system designed and proven in sea trials with a recovery mother ship \citep{zhang2023uuvrecovery}. Recovery of a surface vehicle onto a moving mothership has received far less attention. The authors' own prior controller handles the approach and station keeping with a disturbance observer based model predictive controller and a funnel type robust adaptive control barrier function \citep{park2026recovery}, but that barrier does not certify input admissibility during the terminal entry, and closing that gap is the contribution of this paper.

The kinematics relate the body frame velocity $\nu = [u, v, r]^\top$ to the world frame pose $\eta = [x, y, \psi]^\top$ through the yaw rotation.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_kin}
\dot\eta = R(\psi)\,\nu,\qquad
R(\psi) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The kinetics in the body frame follow the standard maneuvering model.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_kinetics}
M\dot\nu + C(\nu)\,\nu + D(\nu)\,\nu = \tau_c + \tau_w,
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the inertia matrix, $C(\nu)$ the Coriolis matrix, $D(\nu)$ the damping matrix, $\tau_c = [\tau_u, \tau_v, \tau_r]^\top$ the control force and moment, and $\tau_w = [w_u, w_v, w_r]^\top$ the environmental disturbance force. The matrices are given as follows.
\begin{subequations}\label{eq_matrices}
\begin{align}
M &= \begin{bmatrix} m_{11} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m_{22} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & m_{33} \end{bmatrix}, \\[2pt]
C(\nu) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & -m_{22} v \\ 0 & 0 & m_{11} u \\ m_{22} v & -m_{11} u & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \\[2pt]
D(\nu) &= -\begin{bmatrix} X_u & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_v & Y_r \\ 0 & N_v & N_r \end{bmatrix} \nonumber \\
&\quad -\begin{bmatrix} X_{u|u|}|u| & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & Y_{v|v|}|v| & Y_{r|r|}|r| \\ 0 & N_{v|v|}|v| & N_{r|r|}|r| \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $m_{11}=m-X_{\dot u}$, $m_{22}=m-Y_{\dot v}$, and $m_{33}=I_z-N_{\dot r}$ include the added mass and added moment of inertia, the first bracket of $D(\nu)$ collects the linear drag coefficients, and the second collects the quadratic drag coefficients.

The vehicle is actuated by a single stern thruster of thrust $F$ deflected by the rudder angle $\delta$, so the control force and moment are the projection of the thrust onto the body axes.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_actuator}
\tau_c = \begin{bmatrix} \tau_u \\ \tau_v \\ \tau_r \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} F\cos\delta \\ F\sin\delta \\ -\,l_r\,F\sin\delta \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $l_r$ is the distance from the center of gravity to the thruster. The identified parameter values are listed in Table~\ref{tab_params} in Section~\ref{sec_results}.

The rudder and thrust are bounded in amplitude, and their rates are bounded as well, so the admissible input sets are the following.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_amp}
\mathcal{U} = \big\{\, (\delta, F) \,\big|\, \delta_{\min}\le \delta\le \delta_{\max},\ 0\le F\le F_{\max} \,\big\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq_rate}
\dot{\mathcal{U}} = \big\{\, (\dot\delta, \dot F) \,\big|\, \dot\delta_{\min}\le \dot\delta\le \dot\delta_{\max},\ \dot F_{\min}\le \dot F\le \dot F_{\max} \,\big\}.
\end{equation}
Only forward thrust is available, so the vehicle cannot brake directly. A state dependent rate clamp shrinks the admissible rate near the amplitude bounds so that $\mathcal{U}$ is never left. The slow thrust slew is the dominant time scale of the docking, since bringing the thrust from rest to full takes several seconds while the mothership advances at the stream speed $V_s$. The environmental disturbance is confined to a bounded box and is treated as a worst case adversary in the reach avoid design of Section~\ref{sec_control}. The numeric bounds and identified values are collected in Table~\ref{tab_params} in Section~\ref{sec_results}.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec_problem}

The recovery task is stated as follows. The USV approaches from a region alongside the mothership and must enter the cradle aligned with it, without colliding with the mothership hull or the cradle structure, while a bounded environmental disturbance acts on it.

The docking is posed in a cradle fixed frame that advances with the mothership at the stream speed $V_s$, which renders the problem time invariant. The state augments the pose and the velocity with the actuator states, so that the amplitude limits of \eqref{eq_amp} are enforced through the rate limits of \eqref{eq_rate}, giving $s = [x_r, y_r, \psi_r, u, v, r, \delta, F]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^8$ with control the actuator rates $a = [\dot\delta, \dot F]^\top$ and disturbance $w \in \mathcal{W}$ played by an adversary. In this frame the position kinematics carry the constant stream drift.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_relframe}
\begin{aligned}
&\dot x_r = u\cos\psi_r - v\sin\psi_r - V_s, \\
&\dot y_r = u\sin\psi_r + v\cos\psi_r, \quad \dot\psi_r = r,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the actuator states integrate the commanded rates.

The docking target and the collision set are represented by Lipschitz margin functions $\ell$ and $g$.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_margins}
\ell(s)\le 0 \iff s\in\mathcal{T},\qquad g(s)>0 \iff s\in\mathcal{F},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of states inside the cradle pocket with an aligned heading and a gentle relative velocity, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the set of states in which the vehicle collides with the mothership hull or the cradle structure. The concrete geometry of $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ and the numeric thresholds are given in Section~\ref{sec_results}.

The problem is to find a control policy that drives the state into the target while the trajectory stays out of the collision set under every admissible disturbance, that is to attain the robust reach avoid set.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_raset}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{RA}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}) = \big\{\, s \,\big|\, & \exists\,a(\cdot)\ \forall\,w(\cdot)\ \exists\,\tau\ge 0,\ \xi^{a,w}_s(\tau)\in\mathcal{T} \\
& \wedge\ \forall\kappa\in[0,\tau]\ \xi^{a,w}_s(\kappa)\notin\mathcal{F} \,\big\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\xi^{a,w}_s$ is the trajectory from $s$ under control $a$ and disturbance $w$.

Recovery is posed as a reach avoid problem rather than an avoidance problem. The mothership moves, so the recovery window is finite and entering the cradle is itself the mission. If only avoidance were guaranteed, the safest policy would never approach the cradle, and the safe optimal solution would coincide with mission failure. Membership in \eqref{eq_raset} instead certifies that the target \eqref{eq_reach} can be reached while every obstacle in \eqref{eq_avoid} is avoided under any admissible disturbance. The recovery itself is executed in two phases, because underactuation and the narrow cradle prevent a single controller from serving the long range approach and the terminal docking at once, and membership in \eqref{eq_raset} later governs the transition between the phases. The disturbance is kept as the fixed bounded set of Section~\ref{sec_model} and is played by a maximising adversary, rather than being narrowed online by the disturbance observer of the approach phase. During docking the residual after observer compensation is fast, contact driven, and poorly modeled, so a shrinking online estimate would be optimistic exactly when robustness matters most. Optimising against the adversary over the full set yields a value whose sign provides a robust certificate of membership in \eqref{eq_raset}.

The proposed recovery runs in two phases, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_scenario}. Phase 1 covers the approach and dynamic positioning alongside the mothership and is handled by the authors' disturbance observer based model predictive controller with reach avoid control barrier functions \citep{park2026recovery}. That controller estimates and compensates slowly varying environmental loads while a funnel shaped reach avoid barrier keeps the vehicle inside an admissible corridor toward the station keeping point, and its details are given in the forthcoming preprint. Phase 2 is the terminal docking maneuver into the cradle, where the hand designed barrier of Phase 1 offers no guarantee that an admissible input exists under underactuation and actuator saturation, and it is executed by the robust reach avoid policy of Section~\ref{sec_rl}.

The two phases are connected by the supervisor in the state machine of Fig.~\ref{fig_flowchart}, which replaces the earlier dwell time heuristic with a certified handoff. The learned value $V(s)$ of Section~\ref{sec_rl} is negative exactly on the states from which the trained policy can drive the vehicle into the target while avoiding every obstacle under the worst case disturbance, so the supervisor monitors its sign at the current state. Phase 1 hands control to the Phase 2 policy the moment $V(s)<0$, and if the disturbance or an imperfect handoff pushes the state back to $V(s)\ge 0$ before the cradle is entered, control falls back to the Phase 1 station keeping controller and the approach is retried. Membership in the learned reach avoid set thus certifies both the transition and the abort decision with a single scalar test.

The target and avoid sets follow Section~\ref{sec_problem}. The target is the sublevel set $\ell(s)\le 0$ of the target margin in \eqref{eq_margins}, a pocket in cradle relative position and heading combined with a soft gate on the cradle relative velocity, and the failure set is the superlevel set $g(s)>0$ of the collision margin, formed by the cradle walls and the mothership side boundary. The state is augmented with the actuator states $\delta$ and $F$, so the reach avoid set is computed over actuator configurations as well and the optimal policy outputs actuator rates directly. The handoff test then accounts for the thrust and rudder actually available at the switching instant, which matters because a full thrust slew takes about 10~s and dominates the docking timescale.

\subsection{Robust Reach Avoid Reinforcement Learning}\label{sec_rl}
Hamilton Jacobi reachability analyses a dynamical system through a two player differential game. A control input drives the state while a bounded disturbance opposes it, and a value function is assigned to every state so that a set of interest becomes a sublevel set of that value \citep{mitchell2005time, bansal2017hamilton, lygeros2004reachability}. In the avoidance problem the backward reachable tube collects the states from which the disturbance can force the system into a failure set within the horizon, and the value is the outcome of the game in which the control minimises and the disturbance maximises. The safe set is then the region where the value stays nonnegative, and any control that keeps the value nonnegative renders it invariant. This certifies safety but says nothing about attaining a goal, which is why an avoidance only formulation is not enough for docking, where entry into the cradle is the mission.

The reach avoid problem combines the two objectives. The system must reach a target set while never entering a failure set along the way, under the worst case disturbance \citep{margellos2011hamilton, fisac2015reach, hsu2021safety}. With the target margin $\ell$ and the failure margin $g$ of \eqref{eq_margins}, the reach avoid outcome of a trajectory is the payoff, and the value is its optimum over the control. Grid based solvers of this game scale exponentially with the state dimension and are impractical for the eight dimensional model of Section~\ref{sec_model}, which motivates the learning based formulation developed below.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_payoff}
\begin{aligned}
V^{a}(s) &= \min_{\tau\ge 0}\, \max\Big\{ \ell\big(\xi^{a}_s(\tau)\big),\ \max_{\kappa\in[0,\tau]} g\big(\xi^{a}_s(\kappa)\big) \Big\}, \\
V(s) &= \inf_{a} V^{a}(s).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The value is negative exactly on the reach avoid set, that is $V(s)\le 0 \iff s\in\mathrm{RA}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$. Dynamic programming turns \eqref{eq_payoff} into the reach avoid Bellman equation \citep{hsu2021safety}, with $s'$ the next state.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_rabe}
V(s) = \max\Big\{ g(s),\ \min\big\{ \ell(s),\ \inf_{a} V(s') \big\} \Big\}.
\end{equation}
A discount $\gamma\in[0,1)$ makes the backup a contraction \citep{fisac2019bridging, hsu2021safety}. This is the discounted reach avoid Bellman equation.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_drabe}
\begin{aligned}
V_\gamma(s) = {}& (1-\gamma)\max\{\ell(s), g(s)\} \\
&+ \gamma\,\max\Big\{ g(s),\ \min\big\{ \ell(s),\ \inf_{a} V_\gamma(s') \big\} \Big\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Related reachability constrained formulations appear in \citet{yu2022reachability}.

The proposed method extends this discounted backup to the disturbed setting. Under a bounded disturbance the next state depends on both the control and the disturbance, so the future value becomes a differential game in which the protagonist control minimizes and the adversary disturbance maximizes, in the sense of Margellos and Lygeros \citep{margellos2011hamilton}. Replacing the future value operator with the game value yields the robust discounted reach avoid Bellman equation.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_robust}
\begin{aligned}
V_\gamma(s) = {}& (1-\gamma)\max\{\ell(s), g(s)\} \\
&+ \gamma\,\max\Big\{ g(s),\ \min\big\{ \ell(s),\ \inf_{a}\ \sup_{w}\ V_\gamma(s'_{a,w}) \big\} \Big\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $s'_{a,w}$ is the next state under control $a$ and disturbance $w$. Denote the operator on the right by $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$. Two guarantees carry over from the undisturbed case, with proofs deferred to the appendix.
\begin{proposition}
For every $\gamma\in[0,1)$ the operator $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$ is a contraction under the supremum norm, so \eqref{eq_robust} has a unique fixed point $V_\gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{theorem}
For discount factors $0\le\gamma_1\le\gamma_2<1$ the induced sets satisfy $\mathrm{RA}_{\gamma_1}\subseteq\mathrm{RA}_{\gamma_2}\subseteq\mathrm{RA}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$, where $\mathrm{RA}_{\gamma}=\{s\,|\,V_\gamma(s)\le 0\}$, and $\mathrm{RA}_{\gamma}$ converges to $\mathrm{RA}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ in the Hausdorff distance as $\gamma\to 1$.
\end{theorem}
The fixed point is therefore a conservative under approximation of the true robust reach avoid set. A state the value declares recoverable is recoverable under the worst case disturbance up to function approximation error, which is what makes the sign of $V$ a sound certificate for the handoff of Section~\ref{sec_framework}.

The practical algorithm represents the value with twin critics $Q_1, Q_2$ and two actors.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_value}
V(s) = \min\big(Q_1, Q_2\big)\big(s, \pi(s), \mu(s)\big),
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the protagonist actor that minimizes the value and $\mu$ is the adversary actor that maximizes it. The critics regress onto an $N$ step target computed on subtrajectories drawn from the replay buffer and unrolled backward from a bootstrapped tail.
\begin{equation}
y_N = \min\big(Q_1^{-}, Q_2^{-}\big)\big(s_{t+N}, \pi(s_{t+N}), \mu(s_{t+N})\big),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_k = {}& (1-\gamma)\max\big(g_k, \ell_k\big) \\
&+ \gamma\max\Big(g_k, \min\big(\ell_k, y_{k+1}\big)\Big),\ k = N-1, \dots, 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $Q_1^{-}, Q_2^{-}$ are target networks and $\ell_k, g_k$ are the reach and avoid payoffs along the stored subtrajectory. Setting $N=1$ recovers \eqref{eq_robust} in a single step, while larger $N$ folds the reach and avoid information of $N$ consecutive payoffs into one regression target and propagates it backward correspondingly faster. The discount is $\gamma = 0.999$, which gives an effective fold horizon of $1/(1-\gamma)$ steps, about 40~s at 0.2~s per step and therefore longer than the 30~s episode. The factor $(1-\gamma)$ in front of the immediate term is required for the backup to remain a $\gamma$ contraction, and dropping it destroys convergence.

The implementation follows the TD3 recipe. The minimum of the twin critics is used everywhere the value appears, which limits the overestimation that would otherwise inflate the reachable set, target networks are updated by soft Polyak averaging, and both actors are updated at a reduced frequency relative to the critics. The delayed updates proved necessary, since with synchronous updates the actor diverged near the corridor edge where the reach and avoid branches of the backup exchange dominance. Two further training measures were essential. First, the adversary is introduced through a staged disturbance ramp that starts at a zero bound and grows in stages to the full box, so the protagonist first learns a nominal docking maneuver and then hardens it, whereas training against the full adversary from the start collapsed to overly conservative behavior. Second, the mothership side wall is kept in the avoid margin during training, because without it the learned value marked much of the mothership interior as reachable and adding the wall removed these false reachable claims while leaving the genuinely recoverable set essentially intact, as quantified in Section~\ref{sec_results}. A self supervised PDE solver in the DeepReach line \citep{bansal2021deepreach, lin2023generating} was also tried. It drove the Hamilton Jacobi residual to small values and claimed most of the domain reachable, yet the policy extracted from its gradient almost never docked, a mismatch between residual loss and closed loop behavior that motivated the reinforcement learning route, where the value is grounded in rollouts of the very policy being certified. A representative slice of the converged reach avoid set is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_set}, where the zero level of the learned value separates the states from which the policy recovers the vehicle from those it cannot.

\section{Results}\label{sec_results}

The proposed framework was validated in two stages. The docking phase is first evaluated in numerical simulation, where the learned policy is compared against baseline controllers over a Monte Carlo campaign driven by the worst case adversary, and it is then deployed on a processor in the loop setup to confirm real time feasibility and to expose the effects of delay and the zero order hold on embedded hardware. Both stages share the identified Heron USV model of Section~\ref{sec_model}, collected in Table~\ref{tab_params}, and the docking target and collision set defined next. The reach and the avoid conditions are evaluated at a head point $3.0$ m forward of the center of gravity, with position $(x_h, y_h)$, since the bow crosses the cradle mouth first. The target margin takes the box form
\begin{equation}\label{eq_reach}
\begin{aligned}
\ell(s) = \max\big(&|x_h|-C_X,\ |y_h|-C_Y,\ |\psi_r|-\Theta, \\
&|\dot x_r|-U_d,\ |\dot y_r|-U_d\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with pocket half sizes $C_X=4.0$ m and $C_Y=1.75$ m, heading tolerance $\Theta=40$ deg, and a soft docking gate $U_d=1.0$ m/s on the cradle relative velocity, so a state that meets the gate enters nearly at rest with respect to the cradle. The collision set collects the three cradle walls of thickness $0.6$ m and the mothership side boundary, checked at the head point and, for the mothership, also at the center of gravity, and the avoid margin is their pointwise maximum.
\begin{equation}\label{eq_avoid}
g(s) = \max_{i}\, g_i(s).
\end{equation}
The dynamics are discretised at $0.2$ s, a control rate of $5$ Hz, and each episode lasts $30$ s.

\subsection{Numerical simulation}\label{sec_results_sim}

The reach avoid value and the two actors were trained on the shared model with discount $\gamma=0.999$, an $N=5$ step target, a control period of $0.2$ s, and a $30$ s episode, under the disturbance bounds $|w_u|\le 0.089$, $|w_v|\le 0.025$, and $|w_r|\le 0.014$ played by the adversary. The learned policy is compared against three baselines, namely the authors' disturbance observer based MPC with the funnel reach avoid CBF of Phase 1 carried through the docking \citep{park2026recovery}, an ALOS CBF guidance baseline, and a nominal reach avoid policy trained without the adversary. Each controller was subjected to a Monte Carlo campaign of 500 trials with randomized initial conditions inside the learned reach avoid set, and the disturbance was driven by the trained worst case adversary rather than by random noise, so the reported rates reflect performance under the disturbance that the value function was certified against. The metrics are the recovery success rate by disturbance condition, the entry heading and speed error at the pocket, the actuator saturation frequency, the control energy and jerk, the timeout rate, and the computation time.

Under the worst case adversary the proposed policy docked in 467 to 473 of the 500 trials across training seeds, roughly 94 percent, with 1 to 4 of the 500 trials ending in contact with an obstacle and the remainder timing out. The mean docking time was near 19 s, consistent with the thrust slew limit identified in Section~\ref{sec_model}, since a full thrust ramp from rest alone takes about 10 s. The funnel CBF baseline exhibited the failure mechanism that motivated this work. Its actuator saturation frequency grew sharply near the pocket, where the geometric barrier demands lateral corrections that the unactuated sway dynamics cannot deliver, and saturation events preceded the majority of its failures. The nominal policy retained a high success rate under random disturbances but degraded under the adversary, which confirms that robustness must be trained rather than assumed. Representative closed loop trajectories are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_trajectories} and the aggregate Monte Carlo statistics are summarized in Table~\ref{tab_montecarlo}.

The effect of the mothership wall during training was assessed by comparing the learned reach avoid sets with and without it. Without the wall the value function falsely marked about 44.9 percent of the states inside the mothership as reachable, an artifact that would corrupt the certified handoff of Section~\ref{sec_control}. Adding the wall cut this false reachable claim to about 0.1 percent, while the reachable fraction of the free state set only fell from about 47.3 percent to about 44.4 percent, so the correction removed the artifact without materially shrinking the usable set. Value function slices illustrating this comparison are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_wall_ablation}.

\subsection{Processor in the loop simulation}\label{sec_results_pil}

The trained policy was deployed on an embedded processor in the loop setup in which the plant was simulated on a host machine while the policy ran on the target hardware in floating point. The per step inference time of the actor network remained well below the 0.2 s control period at 5 Hz, leaving margin for the remaining guidance and navigation software. Two effects separate this deployment from the training environment. First, the round trip communication delay shifts the state on which the action is computed, and second, the zero order hold keeps the actuator rate command constant over the full period rather than updating it continuously. Because the policy commands actuator rates and the actuator states are part of the augmented state, both effects act as a bounded perturbation that the robust training already covers, and the recovery success rate observed in the processor in the loop campaign remained consistent with the numerical results of Section~\ref{sec_results_sim}. \section{Conclusion}\label{sec_conclusion}

This paper formulated the docking phase of USV cradle recovery as a robust discounted reach avoid problem, extending the discounted reach avoid backup to a bounded disturbance game whose fixed point under approximates the true reach avoid set and converges to it as the discount tends to one, and embedded the learned value in a certified two phase framework in which membership in the reach avoid set decides both the handoff from the approach controller and the fallback out of the docking phase. In Monte Carlo simulations against the worst case adversary the learned policy recovered the underactuated vehicle into the moving cradle with a high success rate and very few collisions, and it transferred to an embedded processor in the loop deployment within the control period, whereas the hand designed funnel barrier failed through actuator saturation under the same underactuation. The certificate is limited by the approximation error of the learned value and policy, and the method has not yet been validated in full scale sea trials, so future work will pursue verified error bounds on the learned set and experimental recovery campaigns at sea.
